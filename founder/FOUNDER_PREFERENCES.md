# Founder Preferences

This document defines practical preferences for how employees should work with the founder.

The goal is to make collaboration efficient and reduce unnecessary friction.

---

# 1. Communication Preferences

The founder prefers communication that is:

- concise
- direct
- structured
- actionable

When communicating, prioritize:

1. What happened?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What do you recommend?
4. What decision is needed?

Avoid:

- long explanations without conclusions
- repeating information already known
- unnecessary context
- presenting problems without possible solutions

---

# 2. Recommendations Over Questions

The founder prefers employees to think before asking.

Do not ask:

> "What should we do?"

Instead:

> "Here are the options. I recommend option B because..."

Employees should research, analyze, and form opinions.

Questions should be used when:

- founder judgment is required
- strategic direction is unclear
- multiple valid paths exist
- important tradeoffs must be chosen

---

# 3. Independence Preference

The founder values autonomy.

Employees should:

- make reasonable decisions independently
- continue progress when possible
- avoid unnecessary interruptions

The founder should not become a bottleneck.

The organization should maximize the amount of progress happening without requiring constant founder input.

---

# 4. Execution Preferences

The founder prefers:

- shipping over endless planning
- simple solutions over unnecessary complexity
- practical progress over theoretical perfection
- learning through execution

Avoid:

- overengineering
- unnecessary abstractions
- building systems without clear value
- spending excessive time polishing before validation

---

# 5. Technical Preferences

When building software:

Prefer:

- simple architectures
- maintainable code
- clear documentation
- appropriate testing
- scalable foundations

Avoid:

- unnecessary complexity
- technology choices without justification
- solutions optimized only for short-term convenience

The best technical decision is the one that creates long-term leverage.

---

# 6. Problem-Solving Style

When encountering problems:

Preferred process:

1. Understand the root cause.
2. Research possible solutions.
3. Compare alternatives.
4. Recommend an approach.
5. Execute.
6. Document learning.

Do not only fix symptoms.

---

# 7. Status Updates

The founder values knowing what employees are doing.

Updates should communicate:

Current state:
- what is happening now

Progress:
- what has been completed

Next step:
- what happens next

Blockers:
- what requires attention

---

# 8. Handling Delays

If blocked:

Do not wait silently.

Communicate:

- what caused the blocker
- what was attempted
- possible solutions
- what decision or resource is needed

---

# 9. Founder Time

Founder time is the organization's most limited resource.

Employees should protect it.

Before asking:

- Can this be researched?
- Can this be tested?
- Can this be decided reversibly?
- Can I create a recommendation?

---

# 10. Preferred Culture

The ideal culture is:

- ambitious
- curious
- entrepreneurial
- accountable
- fast-moving
- continuously improving

Employees should behave like people building something important, not completing isolated tasks.
